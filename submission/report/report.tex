\HWproblem[Predicting the effects of EGFR mutations (G719S, T790M, L858R)]

\HWsubproblem[Map the variants (P1).]\label{p1}
\q{Identify the protein, verify each variant against its sequence, and map all candidate residues to the chosen structure.}
The protein is human EGFR, UniProt P00533 (1{,}210 aa, entry v301); all three variants lie in the kinase domain (712--979) \ext. The stated wild-type residues match UniProt at G719 (first Gly of the GxGxxG P-loop), T790 (gatekeeper) and L858 (three residues after DFG\,855, in the activation segment) \obs.

Each structure's full SEQRES was aligned to UniProt (Biopython, BLOSUM62) and cross-checked against the depositors' SEQADV records (Table~\ref{tab:map}). The key finding is that \textbf{4HJO uses mature-protein numbering, canonical $-24$}: L858 is Leu834 and T790 is Thr766 in 4HJO. In every structure the only mismatches against UniProt are the deposited mutations, including 4HJO's engineered V948R, which keeps the kinase monomeric and inactive~\cite{zhang2006} and lies 26--31\,\AA\ from all three sites. AlphaFold~\cite{jumper2021} pLDDT is 73 (G719), 88 (T790) and 51 (L858); the low L858 value limits confidence in its local model geometry.

\HWsubproblem[Measure structural evidence (P2).]\label{p2}
\q{Define and report reproducible measurements for each candidate.}
Definitions: heavy atoms only; distance = minimum inter-atomic distance; neighbours = residues within 5\,\AA; relative SASA from freesasa (1.4\,\AA\ probe, ligands removed); $\alpha$C state from K745\,NZ--E762\,OE ($<4$\,\AA\ = $\alpha$C-in). Results in 4HJO (Table~\ref{tab:meas}) \obs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T790} is closest to erlotinib (3.48\,\AA), and is the closest of the three sites to the ligand in all six ligand complexes.
\item \textbf{L858} is buried (SASA 0.10, vs 0.15--0.40 in $\alpha$C-in structures), 2.96\,\AA\ from D855 and 4.57\,\AA\ from $\alpha$C residue M766, in a mostly hydrophobic shell that also contains K745, D855 and K860.
\item \textbf{G719} is exposed (SASA 0.35) and 5.06\,\AA\ from erlotinib. In G719S (2ITN), Ser OG is 5.15\,\AA\ from the nearest phosphate (no direct H-bond), and the P-loop moves $\le$1.95\,\AA.
\item \textbf{Met790 check:} apo Met790 (2JIT) superposed on 4HJO appears to clash with erlotinib (0.6--1.3\,\AA), but drug-bound Met790 (4I22) sits 3.3--3.5\,\AA\ from the drug. The clash comes from the apo rotamer (in the active-like 1M17 pocket it is a 2.5\,\AA\ near-contact).
\end{itemize}

\HWsubproblem[Interpret with evidence (P3).]\label{p3}
\q{Compare candidate mechanisms using observed results and cited sources; separate observations from predictions; state uncertainty.}
Table~\ref{tab:egfr} compares the three variants. Ranked by how directly the structures support a mechanism: \textbf{L858R $>$ T790M $>$ G719S}. A single impact ranking is not meaningful, because L858R and G719S are activating mutations that predict inhibitor sensitivity~\cite{lynch2004,paez2004}, while T790M causes acquired resistance~\cite{kobayashi2005,pao2005}. AlphaMissense~\cite{cheng2023} scores all three $\ge$0.96 and cannot separate these classes. Main uncertainties: mechanisms are inferred from static structures that carry V948R, and 4I22 (itself $\alpha$C-out) accommodates Arg858 via salt bridges to D855/D837, which argues against a simple incompatibility model for L858R.

\HWsubproblem[Resolution check (S3).]\label{s3}
\q{Compare at least three Leiden resolutions with other settings fixed; quantify splits/merges and discuss label stability.}
Leiden at 0.3, 0.6, 1.0 and 1.4 on the same graph (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). \textbf{B cells} are perfectly stable (recall and purity 1.00). NK and CD14 monocyte cells always keep their labels, but at low resolution their clusters absorb other cells (the ``NK'' cluster is 62--64\% CD8\,T at 0.3--0.6). CD8\,T, naive and memory CD4 exist only at $\ge$1.0, and at 1.4 the naive/memory boundary moves again. Changing only the seed gives ARI 0.77--0.83, mostly due to Leiden's 2-iteration default (0.94 when run to convergence); almost all of that change is at the naive/memory boundary.

\HWsubproblem[Downsampling check (S4).]\label{s4}
\q{Rerun a fixed-seed 60--80\% sample and compare retained cells quantitatively.}
A 70\% sample (1{,}847 cells, seed 42) re-run from raw counts gives 7 clusters, ARI 0.65 and \textbf{77.6\% label agreement} on shared cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:umap}B). B, NK and monocytes agree at $\ge$0.95. CD8\,T cells split between NK (55\%) and memory CD4 (43\%), and the 8 platelets join the FCGR3A monocytes. Re-clustering at 1.2, or using five other subsample seeds, recovers CD8\,T every time; platelets return in only 1 of 5. Thus the CD8/NK distinction is marker-supported but sensitive to resolution and sampling, while 14 platelets are too few to survive subsampling reliably.

\HWsubproblem[Ambiguous population (S5).]\label{s5}
\q{Investigate one unclear population with marker comparisons and a reason for the uncertainty.}
\textbf{Naive vs memory CD4 (clusters 1 and 0).} The two differ only modestly (cluster 0: S100A4, S100A11, IL32 higher; cluster 1: CCR7, SELL and ribosomal genes higher). The naive-minus-memory score is not bimodal (bimodality coefficient 0.39 $<$ 0.555; AUC 0.73), and 16--21\% of each cluster's kNN edge weight points to the other cluster (Fig.~\ref{fig:s5}) \obs. \emph{Reason} \pred: naive $\to$ central memory $\to$ effector memory is a continuous differentiation path, so the markers form a gradient rather than a gap. Cluster 1 also has a CD8-marker-positive component: 26\% express CD8A/B, versus 3--10\% in B/NK cells and 60\% in the CD8\,T cluster. Normalising these detection rates is consistent with a 31--40\% naive-CD8 contribution, but this is an indirect estimate rather than a definitive cell-type call. Naive CD8 cells share the CCR7/SELL/LEF1 programme, and CD4 mRNA is detected in only 9--12\% of CD4 T cells. We therefore retain the reproducible automatic label ``Naive CD4 T'' while flagging the cluster as ambiguous and potentially mixed; protein data (CITE-seq) would be needed to resolve it.

\textbf{Null:} domain labels permuted across spots on the fixed graph, \textbf{1{,}000 times, seed 0}, keeping domain sizes and the graph. Empirical $p = (1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\text{obs}\})/(N+1)$, one-sided. No permutation came close: null 0.084 $\pm$ 0.003 vs observed 0.783 ($z=224$, $p=0.001$, the minimum possible; Fig.~\ref{fig:null}). A second null that shuffles positions and rebuilds the graph (seed 1) gives the same result and serves as an implementation check. Because depth and \% mito are themselves spatial (Moran's I 0.49, 0.58), we regressed both out and re-clustered: 13 domains, ARI 0.78 with the originals, still coherent (0.792 vs 0.093). \textbf{Limitation:} the null assumes complete spatial randomness, a weak alternative that any smooth gradient would beat; a null preserving autocorrelation was not run. Visium spots also mix cells and share diffused transcripts.
